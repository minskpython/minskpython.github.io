PyCon Belarus

The annual Python conference in Minsk — from the first EventSpace day in 2015 to the free online edition in 2021.

PyCon Belarus & our community

PyCon Belarus (often branded PyConBY) was the largest yearly Python gathering in Belarus: talks and workshops in Russian and English, international speakers, and a home for people who already met each other at local meetups. The live site at by.pycon.org is gone; this page is a community summary drawn from archives and public coverage.

  • Editions: 2015–2021 (final)
  • Organizer: SPACE / EventSpace
  • Venue: Minsk (later online)
  • Archive: Wayback Machine

What it was

Belarus joined the international PyCon family in 2015. The conference was produced by SPACE (EventSpace) in cooperation with Python enthusiasts from Belarus, Russia, Ukraine — and, by 2019, Portugal as well. Talks spanned core Python, web, data science, and open source; many editions ran at SPACE on Kastryčnickaja / Oktyabrskaya in Minsk.

LinkedIn still describes PyCon Belarus as a nonprofit-style annual convention in Minsk with an audience of over 1,500 people across on-site and online formats. Social updates historically lived on facebook.com/pyconby and Twitter @pyconby; the organisational footprint also appears on LinkedIn.

PyCon Belarus was not a Minsk Python Meetup product. SPACE owned production and logistics. The meetup community overlapped heavily as audience, speakers, and programme-committee volunteers — the same people who keep MPM running often helped shape the conference programme.

How the local community took part

Minsk Python Meetup (founded around 2014) and PyCon Belarus grew up in the same city ecosystem: company- and venue-backed meetups through the year, and a flagship conference organized by Event Space. Community organizers and regulars showed up as attendees, speakers, partners, and programme committee members.

A clear public example is 2021: the online edition was announced on the Python mailing list with a free remote event and an open CFP. The archived 2021 site listed a programme committee that included people familiar from the local Python scene (among them Stas Rudakou, Nikita Grishko, Andrii Soldatenko, and others). That is the typical pattern — SPACE stages the conference; local enthusiasts help decide what gets on stage.

Editions, 2015–2021

  1. 2015 31 January · EventSpace, Minsk · first edition

    The first PyCon in Belarus: about 150 people, 14 talks across two tracks, Russian and English, plus lightning talks. PSF Director David Mertz and Austin Bingham were invited keynotes; Wargaming sponsored. Covered by the Python Software Foundation blog. Snapshot: PyCon Belarus'15.

  2. 2016 6 February · SPACE, Oktyabrskaya 16A · 10:00–19:30

    Second year at SPACE. Archived homepage: PyCon Belarus'16. Dates also tracked on pythondeadlin.es.

  3. 2017 4 February · SPACE · 10:00–18:30

    Third edition; bilingual site (Ru/En). Recordings indexed on PyVideo (including speakers such as Armin Ronacher). Snapshot: PyCon Belarus'17.

  4. 2018 24 February · EventSpace · 10:00–18:00 · 4th conference

    Python and Data Science tracks with international speakers (e.g. Tarek Ziadé, Hynek Schlawack, Andrey Svetlov, Gaël Varoquaux) — see DOU calendar. Snapshot: PyCon Belarus'18.

  5. 2019 15–16 February · SPACE, Kastryčnickaja 16/4 · 5th edition

    First two-day format: Junior Day (workshops for newcomers and data science) on 15 Feb, Advanced Day on 16 Feb. Listed on python.org events and DOU; talks on PyVideo 2019. Snapshot: PyCon Belarus 2019.

  6. 2020 21–22 February · Minsk · 6th edition

    Workshops day (21 Feb) and conference day (22 Feb). Speakers on the archived site included Andrew Godwin and Michael Foord, among others. Snapshot: PyConBY 2020.

  7. 2021 13 March · online · free

    Pandemic-era edition: fully remote and free. Line-up on the archived site included Sebastián Ramírez (tiangolo), Mariatta Wijaya, Łukasz Langa, Anthony Shaw, Ian Ozsvald, and many others; programme committee listed publicly. CFP announcement · PyConBY 2021 snapshot.

2021 was the last PyCon Belarus. EventSpace did not run another edition after the free online conference in March 2021. The physical SPACE venue in Minsk had already stopped hosting offline events in spring 2020 because of COVID-19; EventSpace continued some online activity into 2020–2021, then faded — by about mid-2021 the project was no longer visibly active. The series on this page is complete: seven editions, 2015 through 2021.
